The projects page, eight ways

The complaint is the thumbnail: a 16:9 box over a drawing that is nearly square, so every project is letterboxed and the grid loses its rhythm when the new-project tile is taller than the cards beside it. Each layout below answers it differently. Switch Thumbnail to drawing in the panel to see what a real floor plan does to each ratio, and use Only to compare two at a time.

What to decide

  • Ratio. A floor plan is nearly square. Any wide box crops the building away or shrinks it to the middle of a grey field.
  • Is the drawing worth the room? At 248 px wide a plan is a texture, not information. If it cannot be read, a smaller thumbnail or none at all buys a denser list.
  • The new-project tile. It should be the same shape as a card, not a slab that spans two rows.
  • What identifies a project. The name and the numbers are read; the drawing is recognised. Layouts 6 to 8 bet that recognition is not worth a row of its own.
