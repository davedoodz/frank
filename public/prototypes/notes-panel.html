User 1 — GuySenior Architectural Technician · 3D Specialist

1. Architectural programming & regulations

  • Initial client programming and needs assessment takes approximately one day.
  • Regulatory research is a major pain point.
    • Municipal zoning regulations can be difficult to access, especially during revisions.
    • Building codes are difficult to interpret due to ambiguous wording and subjective interpretations.
    • Finding the relevant article is not the problem; understanding and applying it is.
    • Code updates (roughly every four years) create uncertainty about which requirements apply.
  • Regulations are checked initially at a high level, then revisited multiple times as the design progresses.
  • Communication with municipal urban planners is slow and bureaucratic.

2. Design revisions & CCU approvals

  • Waiting for municipal and CCU approvals causes significant delays.
  • Clients frequently request design changes to reduce construction costs.
  • Changes may require renewed discussions with the city and partial or complete resubmissions.
  • Approximately 70% of changes are minor, including some occurring after CCU submission.
  • This creates repeated design and approval cycles.

3. 3D modeling & visualization

  • Benoît sends AutoCAD drawings to Guy, who manually retraces them in SketchUp.
  • Producing high-quality 3D representations and post-production renders is time-consuming.
  • Interactive 3D orbiting could improve client presentations and allow clients to explore individual units.

4. Project tracking & documentation

  • Relies on handwritten notes and memory to track requirements across projects.
  • A checklist with automated red flags and reminders would be valuable.
  • Manually prepares approximately 10 pages of building-code analysis per project.

5. Requested features

  • Zoning and building-code assistance with clear interpretations and article references.
  • Preliminary identification of potential dérogations mineures.
  • Reports that can be shared with municipal urban planners, citing relevant regulations.
  • Project-specific checklists and automated compliance warnings.
  • Interactive 3D unit visualization.
User 2 — BenoîtSenior Architectural Technician · 2D Specialist

1. Drawing revisions & version management

  • Tracking drawing versions and changes is difficult.
  • Changes are sometimes made without documenting why, making past decisions hard to reconstruct.
  • AutoCAD plans are sent to Guy for manual reconstruction in SketchUp.
  • Balcony modifications are a recurring example of design changes.

2. Floor plans & calculations

  • Unit areas are calculated manually using AutoCAD polylines, floor by floor.
  • Would benefit from automated project summaries:
    • Total number of residential units.
    • Unit types (3½, 4½, 5½, etc.).
    • Areas by unit and floor.
    • Window types by floor.
  • Material area calculations for elevations would also be useful.

3. Construction documentation & coordination

  • Coordination with engineers is a major opportunity for improvement.
  • Must manually review mechanical and structural drawings to identify conflicts (e.g., ducts intersecting columns).
  • Would benefit from automatically highlighting aligned elements across floor plans, such as roof drains and columns.
  • Sees greater practical value in these tools during the execution phase than during early design.

4. CCU & client presentations

  • For CCU submissions, area calculations are more useful than detailed 3D functionality.
  • Additional tools during early design could slow down the process.
  • Interactive 3D is primarily valuable for client discussions and spatial understanding.
  • Project PDFs and Photoshop adjustments are part of the presentation/documentation workflow.

Key opportunities for Fitte

  1. Regulatory intelligence: Explain applicable zoning and building-code requirements, with source references and potential compliance flags.
  2. Drawing intelligence: Automate unit, window, and material-area calculations from AutoCAD drawings.
  3. Revision tracking: Identify drawing changes and preserve the reasons behind design decisions.
  4. Cross-drawing coordination: Highlight inconsistencies between floors, disciplines, and 2D/3D representations.
  5. Project management: Provide project-specific checklists, reminders, and approval tracking.

Central insight

Both technicians experience repeated manual verification, but at different stages. Guy’s largest frustrations involve regulatory interpretation and municipal approvals; Benoît’s involve drawing revisions, calculations, and technical coordination. The strongest shared opportunity is reducing repetitive work while preserving design accuracy and traceability.
